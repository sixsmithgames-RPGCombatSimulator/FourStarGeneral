Each entry summarizes the strategic value, potential objectives, force composition (including air and naval support when relevant), terrain and weather conditions, the historical outcome and a confidence score indicating the reliability of the details. The scenarios are sorted chronologically to aid campaign planning.

Detailed Scenarios
Battle of Kasserine Pass (Feb 14-25 1943)

Location: Kasserine Pass, Tunisia

Strategic value: First major clash between US and German armies; testing ground for inexperienced U.S. forces in North Africa and opportunity for Axis to split Allied lines.

Potential objective: For Germany: exploit gaps between U.S. and British forces to seize passes and advance toward supply bases at Tebessa; for U.S. scenario, hold the mountain passes and prevent breakthrough.

Force composition: U.S. II Corps with 1st Armored Division, 1st Infantry Division elements, and supporting artillery; German Fifth Panzer Army spearheaded by 10th and 21st Panzer Divisions, with Luftwaffe air support.

Air and naval support: Limited U.S. close air support from P-38 and P-40 fighters; German Luftwaffe Stuka and fighter-bomber attacks; no naval forces.

Terrain and weather: Narrow mountain passes flanked by rocky ridges; winter rains turning tracks muddy; low visibility in valleys.

Outcome: German tactical victory in early phase but U.S. combined‑arms defence eventually halted Rommel and forced a retreat, leading to a strategic U.S. victory.

Confidence: 80 out of 100

Battle of El Guettar (Mar 23-Apr 3 1943)

Location: El Guettar, Tunisia

Strategic value: U.S. forces sought to cut German lines and open the road to the Tunisian plain; testing Patton's reorganized II Corps after Kasserine.

Potential objective: U.S. objective: defend against German counterattack, then drive through the El Guettar valley toward Gabès; Germans sought to destroy U.S. forces and re‑establish defensive line.

Force composition: U.S. II Corps led by 1st Infantry Division (‘Big Red One’), with elements of the 1st Armored Division, artillery and tank destroyer battalions; German 10th Panzer Division with tanks and infantry, supported by Italian Littorio and 90th Light divisions.

Air and naval support: U.S. air superiority from XII Air Support Command; P-40s and B-25s attacked German columns; Axis received limited Luftwaffe support; no naval forces.

Terrain and weather: Rugged hills and narrow valley constricting movement; spring weather—cool nights, hot days; dusty conditions.

Outcome: U.S. defensive line repelled repeated German tank assaults and inflicted heavy losses, forcing Axis retreat and proving U.S. capability to fight combined‑arms battles.

Confidence: 78 out of 100

Battle of Hill 609 (Djebel Tahent) (Apr 29-May 1 1943)

Location: Near Chouigui, Tunisia

Strategic value: Capturing the dominant hill opened the route to Bizerte and Tunis and proved U.S. infantry could assault a prepared German position.

Potential objective: U.S. objective: seize the high ground of Hill 609 to outflank German defences and enable final offensive toward Tunis; German objective: hold the hill to delay Allied advance.

Force composition: U.S. 34th Infantry Division and elements of the 1st and 9th Divisions supported by artillery and tanks; German 334th Infantry Division with machine‑gun nests and anti‑tank guns.

Air and naval support: Limited U.S. fighter‑bomber strikes and artillery spotting; no naval involvement.

Terrain and weather: Steep, rocky hill dominating surrounding plain; defenders entrenched in caves; early May weather dry and warm.

Outcome: After costly assaults, U.S. infantry captured the summit, forcing German withdrawal and opening the road to Bizerte.

Confidence: 70 out of 100

Capture of Bizerte (May 7-8 1943)

Location: Bizerte, Tunisia

Strategic value: Seizure of Tunisia’s principal port ended Axis resistance in North Africa and ensured Allied control of Mediterranean sea routes.

Potential objective: U.S. objective: capture the heavily defended port city and link up with British troops closing on Tunis; German objective: delay Allied capture to allow evacuation.

Force composition: U.S. 9th and 34th Infantry Divisions with supporting armor and artillery; German and Italian garrison of several thousand troops with coastal artillery and anti‑aircraft guns.

Air and naval support: U.S. bombers and naval gunfire from Allied warships bombarded harbour defences; Luftwaffe activity minimal.

Terrain and weather: Urban fighting in streets and around port facilities; coastal terrain; clear, warm weather.

Outcome: American troops stormed into Bizerte, overwhelming the garrison; combined with British capture of Tunis, this victory ended the North African campaign.

Confidence: 73 out of 100

Operation Husky – Gela Landings (Jul 10-12 1943)

Location: Gela, Sicily

Strategic value: Opening the Sicilian campaign, the landings provided a base for pushing Axis forces out of Sicily and later invading mainland Italy.

Potential objective: U.S. objective: land the 1st Infantry and 45th Divisions to seize the port of Gela and protect flanks of British forces; German objective: counterattack to throw the invaders back into the sea.

Force composition: U.S. 1st Infantry and 45th Infantry Divisions, reinforced by Rangers and paratroopers; Axis defenders included the Hermann Göring Panzer Division and 15th Panzergrenadier Division with ~150 tanks including Tiger I heavy tanks.

Air and naval support: Allied naval bombardment and carrier air cover; USAAF paratroopers dropped inland; German Luftwaffe strafed beaches; Axis naval forces minimal.

Terrain and weather: Beaches backed by low dunes and farmland; midsummer heat; initial surf hindered landing craft.

Outcome: Despite fierce counterattacks, U.S. infantry held the beachhead and repulsed German tanks, securing Gela and enabling Allied forces to advance inland.

Confidence: 85 out of 100

Battle of Troina (Jul 31-Aug 6 1943)

Location: Troina, Sicily

Strategic value: A key German defensive position in Sicily’s mountainous interior; capturing it opened the road to Messina.

Potential objective: U.S. objective: assault the hilltop town and adjacent ridges; German objective: hold Troina to delay Allied advance and allow orderly evacuation.

Force composition: U.S. 1st Infantry Division and 9th Infantry Division supported by artillery and tanks; German 15th Panzergrenadier Division reinforced by Italian units; both sides dug‑in on ridges.

Air and naval support: Intense Allied air bombardment preceded assaults; naval support limited due to inland location.

Terrain and weather: Rugged mountains, steep ridges, and narrow valleys; hot summer heat; dust hampered visibility.

Outcome: After a week of bloody assaults and counterattacks, U.S. forces seized Troina, forcing German withdrawal toward Messina.

Confidence: 72 out of 100

Operation Tidal Wave (Ploesti Raid) (Aug 1 1943)

Location: Ploesti, Romania

Strategic value: Strategic bombing raid aimed to cripple Axis oil production; involved U.S. bombers confronting heavy German and Romanian air defences.

Potential objective: U.S. objective: destroy oil refineries at Ploesti to disrupt Axis fuel supply; German objective: defend critical industrial site using fighters and flak.

Force composition: U.S. Army Air Forces: over 170 B‑24 Liberator bombers from five bomb groups; German and Romanian forces: Luftwaffe fighters and extensive anti‑aircraft batteries.

Air and naval support: A purely air operation; no naval involvement.

Terrain and weather: Low-level flight over flat plains and industrial complex; summer heat; smoke from fires reduced visibility.

Outcome: Raid caused significant damage to some refineries but at heavy cost: more than 50 B‑24s lost; Axis quickly repaired facilities; limited strategic impact.

Confidence: 65 out of 100

Battle of Brolo (Aug 11-12 1943)

Location: Brolo, Sicily

Strategic value: Amphibious flanking maneuver aimed to cut off the retreating German 29th Panzergrenadier Division along Sicily’s north coast.

Potential objective: U.S. objective: land a battalion behind German lines to block Highway 113; German objective: keep escape route open toward Messina.

Force composition: U.S. 3rd Infantry Division (7th Infantry Regiment) conducting amphibious landing; German 29th Panzergrenadier Division with infantry and anti‑tank guns.

Air and naval support: U.S. naval gunfire supported the landing; limited Luftwaffe interference.

Terrain and weather: Coastal cliffs and narrow beaches; hot August weather.

Outcome: American raiders temporarily blocked the road but were outnumbered and withdrew; Germans continued retreat to Messina.

Confidence: 60 out of 100

Capture of Messina (Aug 16-17 1943)

Location: Messina, Sicily

Strategic value: Seizing Messina completed the conquest of Sicily and opened the Strait of Messina for an invasion of mainland Italy.

Potential objective: Allied objective: race to Messina to prevent large-scale Axis evacuation; German objective: delay Allies long enough to ferry troops and equipment to Italy.

Force composition: U.S. 3rd Infantry Division and 45th Infantry Division approaching from the west; German XIV Panzer Corps conducting orderly rearguard with minimal forces.

Air and naval support: Allied air attacks on ferry convoys; Allied naval patrols sought to interdict evacuation; Luftwaffe limited.

Terrain and weather: Urban and hilly terrain near the strait; summer heat.

Outcome: U.S. forces entered Messina hours after the British 8th Army; the majority of German units had already escaped across the strait.

Confidence: 62 out of 100

Operation Avalanche – Salerno Landings (Sep 9-17 1943)

Location: Salerno, Italy

Strategic value: Amphibious invasion of mainland Italy intended to knock Italy out of the war and secure ports for advancing toward Rome.

Potential objective: U.S. objective: land the 36th and 45th Infantry Divisions to seize Salerno beachhead and link with British forces; German objective: drive Allied forces into the sea.

Force composition: U.S. VI Corps (36th & 45th Divisions) supported by Rangers and paratroopers; German 16th Panzer Division and elements of 26th Panzer Division and 29th Panzergrenadier Division; both sides heavy artillery.

Air and naval support: Allied naval gunfire and close air support; Luftwaffe struck invasion fleet with dive bombers, scoring hits on ships.

Terrain and weather: Narrow beaches backed by mountains; vineyards and olive groves; warm late summer weather.

Outcome: After hard fighting and near-breakthrough by German counterattack, Allied forces held the beachhead; German attacks weakened under Allied air superiority and by Sept 15 they withdrew.

Confidence: 82 out of 100

Battle of San Pietro Infine (Dec 8-17 1943)

Location: San Pietro Infine, Italy

Strategic value: Part of the U.S. drive toward the Gustav Line; capturing this village opened Highway 6 toward Cassino.

Potential objective: U.S. objective: capture San Pietro and surrounding heights; German objective: delay Allied advance using fortified hill positions.

Force composition: U.S. 36th Infantry Division supported by 504th Parachute Infantry Regiment and armor; German 15th Panzergrenadier Division with well‑prepared defences.

Air and naval support: Heavy artillery and air bombardment; no naval support (inland).

Terrain and weather: Mountainous terrain with terraced slopes and stone villages; winter rains and cold made manoeuvre difficult.

Outcome: After ten days of intense fighting, U.S. forces took San Pietro and the dominating ridges, but at high cost.

Confidence: 70 out of 100

Rapido River Crossing (First Battle of Monte Cassino) (Jan 20-22 1944)

Location: Rapido River, near Cassino, Italy

Strategic value: Attempt to breach the Gustav Line and open route to Rome; failure led to prolonged stalemate at Cassino.

Potential objective: U.S. objective: cross the flooded Rapido River under fire and establish a bridgehead; German objective: hold the line and inflict heavy casualties.

Force composition: U.S. 36th Infantry Division with engineer units and tanks; German 15th Panzergrenadier Division and elements of 44th Infantry Division entrenched.

Air and naval support: Artillery preparation and limited air support; no naval involvement.

Terrain and weather: Wide, flooded river with marshy banks; German minefields and barbed wire; cold winter nights.

Outcome: The U.S. assault failed; German defenders repelled the crossings, causing heavy casualties and forcing withdrawal.

Confidence: 66 out of 100

Operation Shingle – Anzio Landings (Jan 22 1944)

Location: Anzio and Nettuno, Italy

Strategic value: Designed to outflank the Gustav Line by landing behind German defences; aimed at eventually capturing Rome.

Potential objective: U.S. objective: land British 1st Division and U.S. 3rd Infantry Division at Anzio to threaten German rear; German objective: contain and destroy the beachhead.

Force composition: U.S. VI Corps: 3rd Infantry Division and U.S. Ranger battalions; British 1st Division; German tripwire defences initially consisted of three engineer companies but were quickly reinforced by units of the German 14th Army.

Air and naval support: Strong Allied naval gunfire and air cover; carrier and land-based aircraft; Luftwaffe air raids on beachhead; no German naval forces.

Terrain and weather: Low-lying reclaimed marshland surrounded by hills; January weather cool and damp.

Outcome: Initial landings achieved surprise with minimal casualties, but cautious expansion allowed Germans to occupy surrounding hills and contain the beachhead.

Confidence: 83 out of 100

Battle of Anzio (overall) (Jan 22-May 25 1944)

Location: Anzio beachhead, Italy

Strategic value: The long struggle pinned down German divisions and diverted resources from the Gustav Line, eventually enabling Allied breakout and capture of Rome.

Potential objective: U.S./Allied objective: expand beachhead and break out toward Albano hills and Highway 6; German objective: contain and eliminate Allied pocket.

Force composition: U.S. VI Corps with 3rd, 45th and 34th Infantry Divisions, 1st Armored Division, and British 1st and 56th Divisions; German 14th Army with elements of 4th Parachute, 3rd Panzergrenadier, 26th Panzer and 362nd Infantry Divisions.

Air and naval support: Allied naval guns and air forces provided sustained support; Luftwaffe bombed harbour and attempted to destroy ships; no significant naval battle.

Terrain and weather: Marshy plains surrounded by hills; heavy winter rains followed by spring mud and heat; malaria and flooding hampered operations.

Outcome: After months of stalemate and heavy casualties, Operation Buffalo (May 23-25) broke through the German line; U.S. forces captured Cisterna and advanced inland but diverted north toward Rome, allowing German 10th Army to escape.

Confidence: 78 out of 100

Battle of Cisterna (Jan 30 1944)

Location: Cisterna di Latina, Italy

Strategic value: Part of the Anzio campaign; Ranger raid intended to cut Highway 7 and disrupt German communications.

Potential objective: U.S. objective: Ranger battalions infiltrate and seize Cisterna; German objective: guard road and destroy Allied infiltrators.

Force composition: U.S. Rangers (1st, 3rd, and 4th Ranger Battalions) supported by 3rd Infantry Division; German 715th Infantry Division and elements of 362nd Infantry Division.

Air and naval support: Limited air support; offshore naval guns provided fire support.

Terrain and weather: Swampy lowland with canals and stone farmhouses; winter weather cold and foggy.

Outcome: German forces ambushed and overran the Rangers; most were killed or captured, halting the raid.

Confidence: 62 out of 100

Battle of Monte Cassino (Operation Diadem) (May 11-26 1944)

Location: Gustav Line, Italy

Strategic value: Final Allied assault to break the Gustav Line and open the Liri Valley, enabling the liberation of Rome.

Potential objective: Allied objective: coordinate attacks by U.S. Fifth Army and British Eighth Army to overwhelm German defences; German objective: hold high ground and delay Allied advance.

Force composition: U.S. II Corps (85th and 88th Infantry Divisions) and French Expeditionary Corps; British 8th Army; German 10th Army including 1st Parachute Division and 15th Panzergrenadier Division.

Air and naval support: Massive artillery and air bombardments on Cassino and the monastery; no naval operations (inland).

Terrain and weather: Mountainous, rocky ridges and river valleys; May weather mild but rain created mud in lower areas.

Outcome: Multinational assaults breached German lines; Polish troops captured Monte Cassino abbey; German forces retreated, leading to Allied advance toward Rome.

Confidence: 75 out of 100

Liberation of Rome (Jun 4 1944)

Location: Rome, Italy

Strategic value: First Axis capital liberated; psychological blow to Axis and victory for Allies before D‑Day.

Potential objective: U.S. Fifth Army objective: enter Rome and secure road network; German objective: delay capture while withdrawing north to prepared lines.

Force composition: U.S. Fifth Army: 3rd Infantry Division, 1st Armored Division, 34th Division and other units; German 14th Army rearguard with scattered infantry and armor.

Air and naval support: Allied tactical air forces roamed the skies; minimal naval involvement.

Terrain and weather: Urban fighting in streets and outskirts; early June warm and dry.

Outcome: U.S. troops entered Rome on June 4 amid German withdrawal; city declared open, minimal resistance; German forces escaped to the north.

Confidence: 72 out of 100

Utah Beach (D‑Day) (Jun 6 1944)

Location: Cotentin Peninsula, France

Strategic value: Part of Operation Overlord; securing Utah Beach allowed the Allies to capture Cherbourg and outflank German coastal defences.

Potential objective: U.S. objective: land the 4th Infantry Division and airborne units to secure causeways and link up with paratroopers inland; German objective: defend beach with coastal artillery and mines.

Force composition: U.S. 4th Infantry Division, 8th Infantry Regiment, 101st Airborne Division; German 709th Static Infantry Division and 243rd Infantry Division with coastal batteries.

Air and naval support: Allied naval bombardment and pre‑landing air strikes; U.S. paratroopers dropped inland; German air response negligible.

Terrain and weather: Wide, sandy beaches with flooded marshes behind; overcast skies and surf but tides allowed landing at wrong location to avoid strongpoints.

Outcome: Landing met light resistance; U.S. forces quickly pushed inland and linked with airborne troops, suffering relatively few casualties.

Confidence: 88 out of 100

Pointe du Hoc (Jun 6 1944)

Location: Cliffs west of Omaha Beach, France

Strategic value: Neutralizing coastal guns commanding Omaha and Utah beaches; a daring Ranger assault became iconic.

Potential objective: U.S. objective: scale 100‑foot cliffs and destroy six 155 mm guns; German objective: defend casemates and eliminate intruders.

Force composition: U.S. 2nd Ranger Battalion (~225 men) led by Lt. Col. James Rudder; German garrison of 352nd Infantry Division artillery battery and infantry.

Air and naval support: Pre‑attack naval and air bombardment; destroyers provided close fire support; no German naval involvement.

Terrain and weather: Sheer cliffs with ladders and ropes; heavy surf and wind hindered landing; smoke and debris.

Outcome: Rangers scaled cliffs under fire, found guns relocated inland, destroyed them, and held the position against counterattacks until relieved.

Confidence: 86 out of 100

Omaha Beach (D‑Day) (Jun 6 1944)

Location: Normandy, France

Strategic value: Most heavily defended U.S. beach on D‑Day; capturing it secured the link between British and American sectors.

Potential objective: U.S. objective: land the 1st and 29th Infantry Divisions to breach German Atlantic Wall; German objective: repel invasion using strongpoints and reserves.

Force composition: U.S. 1st Infantry Division (‘Big Red One’) and 29th Infantry Division supported by Rangers and engineers; German 352nd Infantry Division with coastal batteries and machine‑gun nests.

Air and naval support: Massive naval bombardment and air strikes; heavy seas scattered landing craft; limited effect on German bunkers.

Terrain and weather: Narrow beach overlooked by bluffs and cliffs; shingle, seawall and anti‑tank obstacles; morning fog and rough surf.

Outcome: Initial waves suffered heavy casualties but infantry gradually infiltrated flanks, destroyed strongpoints and secured beachhead by evening.

Confidence: 90 out of 100

Battle of Carentan (Jun 10-13 1944)

Location: Carentan, Normandy, France

Strategic value: Critical to link Utah and Omaha beachheads and secure a deepwater port at Cherbourg.

Potential objective: U.S. objective: capture Carentan and its bridges over the Douve River; German objective: hold the town to prevent Allied connection.

Force composition: U.S. 101st Airborne Division with support from 2nd Armored Division; German 6th Parachute Regiment and elements of 17th SS Panzergrenadier Division.

Air and naval support: Limited naval gunfire; Allied aircraft interdicted German reinforcements; Luftwaffe minimal.

Terrain and weather: Marshy lowlands, causeways and hedgerows; cloudy weather with rain showers.

Outcome: After fierce fighting in hedgerow terrain, U.S. paratroopers captured Carentan; German counterattack was repelled by arriving armor.

Confidence: 77 out of 100

Battle of Cherbourg (Jun 22-29 1944)

Location: Cherbourg, Normandy, France

Strategic value: Capture of a deep‑water port vital for Allied logistics; prevented Germans from using it to deny Allied supplies.

Potential objective: U.S. objective: encircle and seize the fortified port; German objective: hold as long as possible and destroy facilities.

Force composition: U.S. VII Corps (4th, 9th and 79th Infantry Divisions) supported by heavy artillery; German garrison of over 20,000 troops including 77th Infantry and 709th Division.

Air and naval support: Allied naval bombardment and aerial attacks suppressed batteries; Luftwaffe limited.

Terrain and weather: Hedgerows and urban fighting inside forts; thick masonry fortifications; variable summer weather.

Outcome: U.S. forces systematically reduced outlying forts and captured the city, though Germans sabotaged port facilities; repairs allowed supply shipments later.

Confidence: 75 out of 100

Battle of Saint‑Lô (Jul 7-19 1944)

Location: Saint‑Lô, Normandy, France

Strategic value: Key crossroads; its capture cleared the path for Operation Cobra and the breakout from Normandy.

Potential objective: U.S. objective: seize Saint‑Lô and surrounding heights; German objective: defend the city to delay Allied breakout.

Force composition: U.S. XIX Corps (29th, 35th and 30th Infantry Divisions) supported by armor and artillery; German 352nd Infantry Division and remnants of 3rd Parachute Division entrenched in ruins.

Air and naval support: Prior to Operation Cobra, heavy bomber raids devastated the town; limited naval support (inland).

Terrain and weather: Hedgerows (bocage) and urban rubble; hot July weather; dust and smoke hindered visibility.

Outcome: U.S. forces fought brutal street battles and cleared the town; heavy casualties but success set stage for the breakout.

Confidence: 74 out of 100

Operation Cobra (Jul 25-30 1944)

Location: Near Saint‑Lô, Normandy, France

Strategic value: Massive U.S. offensive to break through German lines and unleash maneuver warfare across France.

Potential objective: U.S. objective: use concentrated aerial bombardment to punch a hole in German defences and allow armoured exploitation; German objective: hold hedgerow line and prevent Allied breakout.

Force composition: U.S. First Army (VII and VIII Corps) with infantry and armored divisions; German Panzer Lehr Division and 2nd SS Panzer Division.

Air and naval support: Over 2,000 heavy and medium bombers carpet‑bombed German lines; close air support suppressed German movements; no naval involvement.

Terrain and weather: Hedgerow country with small fields and sunken lanes; late July heat; dust and smoke.

Outcome: Bombardment shattered German defences; U.S. armour surged through gap, leading to rapid liberation of Brittany and collapse of German front.

Confidence: 86 out of 100

Operation Lüttich (Mortain Counterattack) (Aug 7-13 1944)

Location: Mortain, Normandy, France

Strategic value: German counterattack aimed at cutting off American spearheads after Operation Cobra; last attempt to contain Allied breakout.

Potential objective: German objective: drive from Mortain to Avranches to cut U.S. supply lines; U.S. objective: hold ground and crush German panzer thrust.

Force composition: German XLVII Panzer Corps (2nd SS Das Reich, 1st SS Leibstandarte, 17th SS Götz von Berlichingen) with ~300 tanks; U.S. 30th Infantry Division, 35th Infantry Division and 2nd Armored Division supported by air and artillery.
